## Temperature differential test in Clearwater

The temperature differential test, called the Temperature Split Test, is an HVAC diagnostic test that measures the difference in degrees between the air entering a return duct and the cooled air leaving a supply register, giving a technician a direct, numeric reading of how much heat the evaporator coil is removing from the airstream. A properly functioning central air conditioning system produces a temperature split of 16 to 22°F under normal operating conditions. A reading outside that range points to a specific mechanical or refrigerant problem before any component is opened or replaced. ### What the temperature differential test measures

The temperature differential test measures three values: the return air temperature (Input Temperature), the supply air temperature (Output Temperature), and the delta-T. Delta-T is the arithmetic difference between the two. A technician places a calibrated thermometer at the return grille to record the Input Temperature, then moves to the nearest supply register to record the Output Temperature. Subtracting the supply reading from the return reading produces the differential range in degrees Fahrenheit. That single number tells the technician whether the evaporator coil is absorbing heat at the rate the system was designed to achieve. The Delta-T Test is fast. The two readings and the subtraction take under five minutes, yet the result carries more diagnostic weight than a visual inspection alone, because it quantifies performance rather than describing appearance.

### Normal range and how to read temperature differential test results

Temperature differential test results fall into three zones. A split of 16 to 22°F confirms the system is transferring heat at the correct rate. A split below 16°F means the coil is not absorbing enough heat, which typically points to low refrigerant charge, a dirty evaporator coil, or restricted airflow across the coil. A split above 22°F means the return air is losing too much heat before it reaches the coil, or that airflow volume is too low, which forces the coil to over-cool a small volume of air. Both failure zones produce discomfort and higher electricity use even when the compressor runs continuously. Humidity in Clearwater's climate makes a low split especially noticeable, because an underperforming coil removes less moisture from the air.

### Component parts of the test

The supply air reading is taken at the register closest to the air handler, where the air has not yet mixed with room air and the temperature is at its lowest point in the duct run. The return air reading is taken at the return grille before the air enters the filter and coil, capturing the true load temperature the system must handle. The delta-T calculation converts two raw numbers into a single actionable value. All three parts must be measured under stable operating conditions. The system should run for at least 15 minutes before readings are taken, and doors and windows should be closed so the load is representative of normal use.

### How the temperature differential test relates to the broader AC system

The temperature differential test is a type of HVAC diagnostic test, placing it in the same category as static pressure testing and refrigerant pressure checks, but it differs from those tests in what it directly measures. A Static Pressure Test measures resistance to airflow inside the duct system. It does not reveal coil heat-transfer efficiency on its own. A Visual Inspection identifies physical damage, ice buildup, or debris but produces no numeric performance value. The Temperature Split Test bridges the gap: it uses the duct system's own airstream as the measurement medium, so the result reflects the combined effect of refrigerant charge, airflow volume, coil cleanliness, and ambient load in one number.

The test applies to any system that moves conditioned air through ducts, including split systems installed during [central air conditioning installation](https://poulincoolingacandrefrigeration.mintlify.app/central-air-conditioning-installation) and systems built into [new construction AC installation](https://poulincoolingacandrefrigeration.mintlify.app/new-construction-ac-installation) projects. A licensed [air conditioning contractor](https://poulincoolingacandrefrigeration.mintlify.app/air-conditioning-contractor) uses the delta-T reading alongside refrigerant pressures and static pressure readings to build a complete picture of system health before recommending any repair.

### Common problems the test identifies

A temperature differential test result below 16°F is the most common finding on a system that runs but does not cool adequately. Low refrigerant charge is the leading cause. When refrigerant level drops, the evaporator coil operates at a higher suction pressure and absorbs less heat per pound of airflow. A dirty evaporator coil produces the same symptom by insulating the refrigerant from the airstream. Restricted return airflow, caused by a clogged filter, a closed damper, or undersized return grilles, reduces the volume of warm air reaching the coil, which collapses the split. A split above 22°F is less common. It points to severely restricted supply airflow, often from closed registers or a blower motor running below its rated speed. Identifying which cause is present requires combining the delta-T reading with refrigerant pressure data and an airflow measurement, which is why the Temperature Drop Test is a starting point in a diagnostic sequence, not a standalone conclusion.

### Conducting a temperature differential test: steps and conditions

Conducting a temperature differential test correctly requires stable system operation, accurate instruments, and consistent measurement locations. The system must run for at least 15 minutes before readings are taken so temperatures stabilize. Outdoor temperature affects the result. Tests performed when the outdoor temperature is below 65°F or above 100°F may produce splits that fall outside the 16 to 22°F reference range even on a properly functioning system, because the refrigerant circuit operates at pressures outside its design envelope at those extremes. A calibrated digital thermometer with a probe tip reads more accurately than an infrared gun at a supply register, because supply air velocity causes infrared sensors to read low. The return air reading is taken at the face of the return grille, not inside the duct, to avoid measuring duct wall temperature.
