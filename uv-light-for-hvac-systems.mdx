## UV light for HVAC systems in Clearwater - Poulin Cooling A/C and Refrigeration

UV light for HVAC systems. Called an HVAC UV sterilizer. Is a type of Indoor Air Quality (IAQ) equipment. It mounts a UV-C emitting lamp inside a forced-air system to neutralize airborne pathogens, mold spores, and bacteria before they circulate through living spaces. The lamp sits inside the air handler or ductwork, exposing passing air and coil surfaces to germicidal ultraviolet radiation at a wavelength of 254 nm, which disrupts the DNA of microorganisms and prevents them from reproducing. Most residential installations draw between 20 and 75 watts. The lamp runs continuously whenever the air handler operates, making it a low-energy addition to an existing system.

### What UV light for HVAC systems is

UV light for HVAC systems is germicidal IAQ equipment, not a filter. A standard air filter captures particles mechanically by trapping them in a fibrous medium; a duct UV light destroys the cellular structure of pathogens that pass through the light field, including organisms small enough to pass straight through even a high-MERV filter. The two approaches address different threats, which is why a UV air purifier for HVAC is often paired with a [whole-home air filtration system](https://poulincoolingacandrefrigeration.mintlify.app/whole-home-air-filtration-system) rather than replacing it. The core components are a UV-C bulb, a ballast that regulates power to the lamp, and a mounting bracket that positions the bulb at the correct distance from the evaporator coil or duct wall. ### Measurable attributes of UV light for HVAC systems

UV light for HVAC systems operates in the UVC band, spanning 200 to 280 nm. Most germicidal lamps are tuned to 254 nm because that wavelength delivers high DNA-disruption efficiency for common airborne pathogens. Irradiance intensity, measured in µW/cm², determines how much germicidal energy a passing microorganism actually receives. A lamp with insufficient irradiance at the coil surface will not achieve meaningful kill rates, regardless of its wattage rating. Lamp lifespan runs between 8,000 and 12,000 hours depending on bulb type. Roughly one to two years of continuous operation before output degrades below effective levels. Power draw ranges from 20 to 75 watts. Even the larger commercial-grade units fall well within the capacity of a standard HVAC electrical circuit. ### How UV light for HVAC systems relates to the broader HVAC system

UV light for HVAC systems is a component part of the broader HVAC system, installed during AC installation or added as an upgrade to an existing air handler. ### UV light for HVAC systems installation and placement

Proper placement is critical. UV light for HVAC systems installation requires positioning the lamp so the irradiance field covers the full face of the evaporator coil or the cross-section of the return duct, depending on the application. Distance from the coil surface matters because irradiance follows an inverse-square relationship with distance: doubling the distance from the lamp to the target surface cuts effective irradiance to roughly one quarter. A licensed technician selects lamp wattage and bracket position based on the air handler cabinet dimensions and the coil face area, not a one-size-fits-all mount. Electrical connection ties into the air handler's low-voltage circuit so the lamp cycles with the blower, preventing unnecessary UV exposure when the system is off. ### UV light for HVAC systems maintenance

UV light for HVAC systems maintenance centers on one task: replacing the UV-C bulb before output falls below effective irradiance levels. Output degrades before the lamp stops glowing. A lamp that has exceeded its rated 8,000 to 12,000 hour lifespan may still produce visible light but delivers little germicidal UV-C output, creating a false sense of protection. Bulb replacement is typically scheduled annually in systems that run year-round, which is standard in Clearwater's climate.
