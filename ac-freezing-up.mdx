## AC freezing up in Clearwater

AC Freezing Up, called Air Conditioner Freeze or a frozen AC coil, is a type of Air Conditioner Problem where ice forms on the evaporator coil inside the air handler. This ice blocks heat transfer, restricts airflow through the duct system, and eventually shuts down the unit's ability to cool the home. The condition develops when the coil surface drops below 32°F, causing moisture in the air to freeze on contact rather than drain away as condensate. Ice is the problem.

### Components involved in AC freezing up

The evaporator coil serves as the primary site where ice forms in AC freezing up. AC Freezing Up affects several distinct parts of the air conditioning system beyond the coil itself. The air filter, condensate drain line, refrigerant line and blower fan all play a role in whether the condition develops or worsens. A clogged air filter is one of the most common starting points because it cuts the volume of warm air reaching the coil, leaving the coil surface cold enough to freeze. The condensate drain line matters too: once ice melts during a thaw cycle, a blocked drain causes water to back up and overflow the drip pan. ### Measurable attributes of a frozen AC coil

A frozen AC coil carries specific, measurable values that distinguish it from normal operation. The evaporator coil surface temperature falls below 32°F, which is the threshold at which moisture freezes rather than evaporates. Ice formation thickness on the coil ranges from about 0.25 inches in early-stage freezing to 2 inches or more when the condition has been running unchecked for hours. Airflow restriction is measurable as a CFM drop greater than 40 percent compared to the system's rated capacity. Refrigerant low-side pressure drops below the normal operating range when a refrigerant leak is the root cause, which a technician reads directly off the service gauges. [System diagnostics](https://poulincoolingacandrefrigeration.mintlify.app/system-diagnostics) tie these pressure, temperature and airflow readings together to identify whether the freeze is caused by restricted airflow, low refrigerant or a combination of both.

### How AC freezing up relates to the broader AC system

AC Ice Up is a fault condition within the refrigeration cycle, not a standalone failure. The evaporator coil is designed to absorb heat from indoor air; when airflow or refrigerant charge falls outside the design range, the coil cannot absorb enough heat to stay above freezing. This connects the problem directly to the compressor, which continues running and pumping refrigerant even as ice blocks the coil, eventually overworking the compressor and risking permanent damage. AC Freezing Up contrasts sharply with normal cooling operation, where the coil stays between roughly 35°F and 55°F, condensate drips freely into the drain pan and airflow moves through the coil at the rated CFM. It contrasts with AC overheating, which occurs on the outdoor condenser side when the refrigerant charge is too high or the condenser coil is dirty, driving high-side pressure up rather than low-side pressure down. Understanding where AC Freezing Up sits in the system helps explain why a thaw alone is never a fix: the underlying cause remains active the moment the ice melts and the unit restarts. Systems that were installed without proper load calculations are more vulnerable to this cycle, which is why correct sizing at the time of [central air conditioning installation](https://poulincoolingacandrefrigeration.mintlify.app/central-air-conditioning-installation) reduces the long-term risk of freeze events.

### Signs and common causes of AC freezing up

Frost accumulation on the refrigerant line running from the air handler to the outdoor unit is often the first visible sign a homeowner notices. Other signs include warm air blowing from the supply vents despite the thermostat calling for cooling, water pooling around the base of the air handler as ice melts, and a complete loss of airflow when ice has fully blocked the coil face. Two causes are most common. Restricted airflow stems from a dirty air filter, closed supply registers, a failing blower motor, or a collapsed return duct. Low refrigerant charge points to a leak somewhere in the refrigerant circuit, since refrigerant does not deplete on its own. A third cause, less common but worth noting, is running the air conditioner when outdoor temperatures fall below about 60°F, which reduces the heat load on the coil enough to cause freezing even with a clean filter and correct charge. ### AC freezing up fix and repair process

An AC freezing up fix starts with a full thaw before any diagnosis or repair begins, because gauges and airflow readings taken with ice on the coil are not accurate. The thaw takes 1 to 24 hours depending on ice thickness, and the system must stay off or run in fan-only mode during that time. Once the coil is clear, the technician measures low-side refrigerant pressure, checks static pressure across the coil, inspects the air filter and blower, and examines the condensate drain. If low refrigerant pressure confirms a leak, the technician locates and repairs the leak before adding refrigerant. If restricted airflow is the cause, the fix is replacing the filter, clearing the duct obstruction or repairing the blower. New construction projects carry a higher risk of debris-related airflow restriction in the first season of operation, which is one reason post-installation inspection matters after [new construction AC installation](https://poulincoolingacandrefrigeration.mintlify.app/new-construction-ac-installation). A properly repaired system returns low-side pressure to the manufacturer's specified range and coil temperature to the 35°F to 55°F operating band. ### Where AC freezing up occurs

AC Freezing Up occurs in split-system air conditioners, packaged units, and ductless mini-split systems wherever an evaporator coil is present. In Clearwater, FL, the condition appears most often during the summer cooling season. Systems run continuously for long stretches, and any restriction or refrigerant loss compounds quickly. High humidity accelerates ice buildup because there is more moisture in the air to freeze on the coil surface. Attic-mounted air handlers are a common location for the problem in Florida homes, where poor insulation and high ambient attic temperatures can mask early signs until ice has already formed. New equipment is not exempt. A new system with an undersized return duct or an incorrect refrigerant charge at startup can freeze within the first cooling season.
