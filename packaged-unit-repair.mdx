## Packaged unit repair in Clearwater - Poulin Cooling A/C and Refrigeration

Packaged unit repair, called RTU Repair, is the diagnosis and restoration of single-cabinet air conditioning systems where the compressor, condenser coil, evaporator coil and blower fan all live inside one self-contained housing, typically mounted on a rooftop or a concrete pad beside a building. [Poulin Cooling Air Conditioning and Refrigeration](https://maps.app.goo.gl/pVu4x7kW5wjrB2w18) handles packaged unit repair throughout Clearwater and the surrounding area.

### Scope of work and component parts in packaged unit repair

Packaged unit repair covers every major component inside the cabinet: the compressor, condenser and evaporator coils, blower fan motor, capacitors, contactors, refrigerant circuit and electrical controls. A technician begins with a full system diagnostic to isolate the fault before any part is touched. [System diagnostics](https://poulincoolingacandrefrigeration.mintlify.app/system-diagnostics) on a packaged unit typically include pressure testing the refrigerant circuit, measuring amperage draws on the compressor and fan motors, checking capacitor microfarad ratings and verifying thermostat and control-board signals. After diagnosis, the scope shifts to parts replacement, refrigerant recharge and system testing to confirm the repair holds under load.

Packaged AC Repair is a type of HVAC Repair, sitting within the broader category of commercial and residential cooling service. Because all mechanical components share one cabinet, a fault in one part often stresses adjacent components, so a thorough scope of work checks the whole assembly rather than only the failed part.

### Measurable attributes of packaged unit repair

Packaged unit repair applies to equipment with cooling capacities ranging from 2 to 25 tons. This covers small retail spaces up to large commercial rooftop installations. SEER ratings on units in this class run from 13 to 16, and the refrigerant charge is R-410A or R-32 depending on the equipment model. A typical repair visit runs 2 to 6 hours from arrival to system-on confirmation. Compressor replacements on larger tonnage units can extend that window. Warranty periods on replacement compressors and coils vary by manufacturer, so a technician confirms the specific term before ordering parts. Required tools include refrigerant recovery and recharge equipment, a digital manifold gauge set, a clamp meter, a capacitor tester, and a combustion analyzer for units with gas-heat sections.

### Common problems and signs that packaged unit repair is needed

Packaged unit repair is needed when the system shows warm air at the supply registers, short cycling, loud grinding or rattling from the rooftop cabinet, ice on the coil, tripped breakers, or a complete failure to start. Refrigerant leaks are among the most frequent causes. A low charge forces the compressor to run longer cycles, raising energy use and accelerating wear. Capacitor failure is another common fault: a weak capacitor prevents the compressor or fan motor from starting, which reads as a no-cool call but resolves quickly once the part is swapped. Dirty condenser coils raise head pressure. This pushes the compressor toward overheating, so coil cleaning is often part of the repair scope even when it was not the original complaint.

### How packaged unit repair relates to the broader HVAC system

Packaged unit repair is distinct from split system repair and ductless mini-split repair because the entire refrigerant circuit is contained in one outdoor cabinet rather than split between an indoor air handler and an outdoor condenser. That single-cabinet design simplifies access for a technician but means a refrigerant leak or electrical fault affects heating and cooling simultaneously when the unit includes a heat section. Packaged unit repair is different from packaged unit replacement and packaged unit installation: repair restores the existing equipment, while replacement and installation involve selecting and setting a new unit. Self-Contained AC Service is most common in light commercial buildings, retail strip centers, restaurants, schools, and low-rise office buildings where rooftop units are the standard installation. Residential applications exist too. This is particularly true in homes built on slabs in Florida, where a ground-mounted packaged unit replaces the traditional split system.
