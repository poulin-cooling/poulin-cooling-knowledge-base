A Florida AC Contractor License, called a CAC License Florida, is the official state authorization permitting a contractor to legally perform air conditioning contracting services in Florida. It covers installation, repair, replacement, and maintenance of cooling systems in residential and commercial properties. The license is a type of Florida Contractor License, issued and regulated by the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation. Without it, any AC work performed for compensation is classified as unlicensed AC work. That classification carries civil and criminal penalties under Florida law.

What the Florida AC contractor license is

The Florida AC Contractor License is a state-issued credential that authorizes a contractor to enter into agreements for, and to perform, air conditioning system work throughout Florida. Two primary license types exist under this framework: the CAC (Certified AC Contractor) license, which is valid statewide, and the RAC (Registered AC Contractor) license, which is valid only within the jurisdiction of the local authority that issued it. The Florida HVAC Contractor License falls under the same regulatory structure and shares the same issuing authority. Air conditioning contractors in Clearwater operate under the CAC designation to serve customers across county lines without restriction.

Measurable attributes of the Florida CAC license

The Florida AC Contractor License carries several specific, verifiable attributes that distinguish a valid credential from an expired or suspended one. These attributes are the basis for any license verification and are the first things a homeowner or property manager should check before hiring a contractor.

  • License type: License type is either CAC (Certified AC Contractor, statewide) or RAC (Registered AC Contractor, local jurisdiction only), and the type determines where the contractor may legally work.
  • Issuing authority: Issuing authority is the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation, the single state body that grants, renews, and disciplines all Florida AC contractor licenses.
  • License number: License number is an alphanumeric identifier assigned at issuance; it appears on every permit pulled and every contract signed, and it is the primary field used to verify Florida AC Contractor License status online.
  • Expiration date: Expiration date is formatted MM/DD/YYYY and marks the end of the current two-year renewal cycle; a license past this date is not valid for new work.
  • Renewal cycle: Renewal cycle is 24 months, and each cycle requires 16 hours of continuing education before the DBPR will process the renewal.
  • Status: Status is recorded as active, inactive, or suspended; only an active status authorizes the contractor to pull permits and perform work.

Florida AC contractor license requirements

Florida AC Contractor License requirements include passing a state examination, demonstrating financial responsibility, and carrying the required insurance before the DBPR issues the credential. The examination tests knowledge of refrigeration principles, load calculations, electrical codes, and Florida-specific building regulations. Financial responsibility documentation, such as a surety bond or proof of net worth, must accompany the application. Renewal is not automatic. Continuing education of 16 hours per two-year cycle is mandatory, and failure to complete it results in an inactive or lapsed status. These Florida CAC license requirements apply uniformly to every applicant regardless of prior experience or out-of-state credentials.

How the Florida AC contractor license relates to the broader licensing system

The Florida AC Contractor License sits within the broader Florida Contractor License hierarchy administered by the DBPR. It is a specialty contractor license. That designation means it authorizes a defined scope of work rather than general construction. The CAC license specifically authorizes the licensee to install, service, and replace air conditioning systems, including refrigerant handling, which itself requires EPA Section 608-certified technicians under federal law. The license connects directly to the permit system: Florida building departments require a valid CAC or RAC license number on every mechanical permit application before they will issue the permit. A contractor without an active license cannot legally pull a permit. Work done without a permit may require demolition and reinstallation at the property owner's expense.

Unlicensed AC work and what it means for property owners

Unlicensed AC work is any air conditioning installation, repair, or replacement performed for compensation by a contractor who does not hold an active Florida AC Contractor License. Unregistered contractor status and unlicensed AC contractor activity are treated as violations under Florida Statute 489. Penalties range from fines and stop-work orders to criminal charges in repeat cases. The consequences for property owners extend beyond the contractor. Insurance claims related to AC systems installed or repaired by an unlicensed contractor may be denied, and unpermitted work must be disclosed during a property sale. Verifying a contractor's license before signing any agreement is the most direct protection against these outcomes. The DBPR's online search tool allows anyone to verify Florida AC Contractor License status in real time using the contractor's license number or business name.
