Scope and component parts of a noise assessment

A noise assessment is a type of Diagnostic Service performed on an AC system to detect malfunctions that produce abnormal sound. A professional noise assessment uses a sound meter to capture measured sound pressure level in dB SPL at multiple points around the unit, the air handler and the ductwork. The technician then produces an analysis report that documents each reading alongside the source location, and a written technician's evaluation ties those readings to a specific component or airflow condition. A AC inspection often runs alongside the acoustic work so mechanical findings and sound findings appear in the same visit record. Technicians rely on system diagnostics data to interpret the collected readings accurately.

Noise assessment produces three primary measurements that define the scope of any sound problem. The attributes and their units are listed below.

  • Decibel level (dB): Decibel level is the measured sound pressure level at a defined distance from the unit, recorded in dB SPL.
  • Frequency range (Hz): Frequency range is the measured frequency range of the dominant sound, expressed in Hz.
  • Measurement duration (minutes to hours): Measurement duration is the length of time the technician records data, which ranges from a few minutes for a steady-state tone to several hours for an intermittent noise that only appears under specific load conditions. Intermittent sounds are the hardest to capture and are the main reason measurement duration matters as a documented attribute.

Noise assessment relation to the broader AC diagnostic system

A noise assessment sits inside the broader category of AC diagnostic services, connecting directly to the inspection and repair workflow. Sound data from an Acoustic Analysis narrows the field of possible faults before a technician opens the cabinet, which shortens the diagnostic time. Noise assessment reports are used alongside refrigerant pressure readings, electrical measurements and airflow data to build a complete picture of system health. The noise assessment report is the document that links the acoustic finding to the repair recommendation, making it the connective tissue between the sound survey and the actual fix. Noise assessment versus noise mitigation and quiet operation

Noise assessment is the measurement and identification phase. It is not noise mitigation or quiet operation. Noise mitigation is the corrective work that follows a completed assessment, covering vibration isolation pads, duct lining, compressor blankets, and component replacement. Quiet operation is the end state a system reaches after mitigation is complete. AC repair is a related but separate service that addresses the mechanical fault a noise assessment identifies. Without a completed Sound Level Survey first, mitigation work lacks a documented dB SPL baseline to confirm the repair actually reduced the sound to an acceptable level.
