What AC repair is and what it is not

Air conditioning repair is the targeted correction of a specific fault in an existing system. It resolves an AC breakdown without replacing the equipment. That distinction matters: AC replacement removes and swaps the entire unit, while system installation puts new equipment into a space that had none. Repair keeps the existing system running when the fault is isolated and the core components are still sound. A licensed air conditioning contractor evaluates whether the problem warrants repair or replacement before any work begins.

Common signs that AC repair is needed

Air conditioning repair becomes necessary when the system shows measurable performance loss or stops operating entirely. The signs below point to specific faults rather than general wear.

  • Warm air from supply vents: Warm air from supply vents points to low refrigerant charge, a failed compressor, or a restricted metering device.
  • Short cycling: Short cycling, where the unit turns on and off every few minutes, often traces to an oversized unit, a dirty evaporator coil, or a failing capacitor.
  • Ice on the evaporator coil: Ice on the evaporator coil signals restricted airflow or a refrigerant leak, both of which stop cooling immediately.
  • Unusual sounds: Grinding, squealing, or banging sounds indicate a mechanical fault in the blower motor, fan blade, or compressor.
  • High indoor humidity: High indoor humidity despite the system running suggests the evaporator coil is not removing latent heat, often because airflow is too low.
  • Tripped breaker or no power: A tripped breaker that resets and trips again points to an electrical fault that needs diagnosis before the unit runs again.

Ignoring these signs extends the fault window and can turn a minor repair into a compressor replacement. Refrigerant work, in particular, requires EPA 608-certified handling, so a licensed technician should address any suspected leak.

AC repair cost factors and when replacement makes more sense

AC repair cost depends on the specific component that failed, the refrigerant type the system uses, and the labor time required for access and testing. Older systems running R-22 refrigerant carry higher service costs. R-22 is no longer manufactured domestically, and supply is limited. A general industry guideline compares the repair cost against the age of the unit: a repair that approaches or exceeds half the cost of a new system on equipment older than 10 to 12 years often makes replacement the better financial decision.
