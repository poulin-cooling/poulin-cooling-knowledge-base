EPA certification for refrigerant handling in Clearwater - Poulin Cooling A/C and Refrigeration

EPA Certification for Refrigerant Handling, called Section 608 Certification, is a professional credential that authorizes individuals to legally purchase, recover, recycle, and dispose of refrigerants used in stationary air conditioning and refrigeration equipment. It sits within the broader category of trade licensing that governs who may work on systems containing controlled substances under federal environmental law. The certification divides into four distinct types based on the equipment a technician services. Type I covers small appliances. Type II covers high-pressure systems, Type III covers low-pressure systems, and Universal covers all three. Requirements can vary and should be confirmed with the EPA. Scope and parts of the EPA certification process

EPA Certification for Refrigerant Handling is earned by passing a proctored exam administered by an EPA-approved certifying organization. The exam structure contains a core section and one or more type-specific sections: the core exam tests general refrigerant regulations and safety, while the Type I, Type II and Type III exams each test the equipment category the technician intends to service. Measurable attributes: type, validity and renewal requirements

The certification type number corresponds directly to the equipment category: Type I, Type II, Type III or Universal. Universal is the most common credential held by full-service HVAC technicians because it covers the widest range of equipment, including the high-pressure systems found in most residential and light commercial AC units. System diagnostics on refrigerant-containing equipment must follow applicable regulations.

Where the refrigerant handling certification applies in practice

The refrigerant handling certification applies to any stationary air conditioning or refrigeration system that uses a regulated refrigerant, which includes virtually all residential split systems, packaged units, mini-splits, commercial rooftop units and refrigeration cases. Mobile vehicle air conditioning systems fall under a separate Section 609 rule and are not covered by the Section 608 credential.
