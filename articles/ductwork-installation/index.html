Ductwork installation is the process of designing and installing new duct runs to distribute conditioned air throughout a building. It is a type of HVAC installation procedure. The work falls under the broader category of air distribution work. A new ductwork install covers trunk lines, branch runs, and register boots, sized and routed to match the specific airflow demands of each room. Duct installation is performed during a new AC system build-out or when an existing air distribution layout no longer fits the structure.

What ductwork installation is and what it includes

Ductwork installation is the fabrication and placement of a complete air distribution network inside a home or commercial building. The work begins with a Manual D calculation. That calculation sets the required CFM for every supply and return run before a single piece of material is cut. Trunk lines carry the bulk of the airflow from the air handler, branch runs split off to individual rooms, and register boots connect those runs to the grilles visible in the ceiling or floor. A properly sized and routed system delivers the right volume of air to every space without straining the equipment.

Ductwork installation is performed during AC installation, making it one of the first physical steps in a full system build. Poulin Cooling A/C handles ductwork fabrication as part of complete system installs in Clearwater and the surrounding area.

Duct materials used in HVAC duct installation

HVAC duct installation relies on three primary materials, each suited to different parts of the system. The choice affects airflow efficiency, durability, and how well the finished runs hold their shape over time.

  • Sheet metal: Sheet metal is the most rigid option and holds its shape permanently, making it the standard choice for trunk lines and long straight runs where pressure loss must stay low.
  • Flexible duct (flex): Flex duct connects branch runs to register boots and navigates tight spaces that rigid metal cannot reach, though every unnecessary bend adds resistance to airflow.
  • Fiberglass duct board: Fiberglass duct board is cut and assembled on-site into rectangular sections and provides built-in insulation, which reduces condensation in humid climates like Clearwater.

After new duct runs are installed, the joints and seams need to be sealed to prevent conditioned air from escaping into unconditioned spaces. Duct sealing is a distinct follow-on step, not part of the fabrication itself.

Sizing basis and job duration for new ductwork installs

New ductwork installs are sized using Manual D, the ACCA standard that calculates required CFM for each room based on heat gain, heat loss, and the layout of the structure. Undersized ducts restrict airflow and force the air handler to work harder. Oversized ducts reduce velocity and cause uneven temperatures across rooms. Getting the sizing right at the design stage is what separates a system that performs from one that runs constantly without cooling the space.

A typical residential ductwork installation takes one to two days to complete, depending on the size of the home and the complexity of the routing. Finished ceilings and limited attic access extend that timeline. Installers must work around existing structure in those conditions, which adds time to each run. Clearwater's high humidity means every run in an unconditioned attic needs proper insulation wrap to prevent condensation from forming on the duct surface.

Excess moisture in the structure is a separate concern that a dehumidifier addresses after the system is running.

How ductwork installation relates to the broader HVAC system

Ductwork installation is the air distribution layer that connects the air handler to every conditioned space in the building. Without a properly designed duct network, even a correctly sized AC unit cannot deliver its rated capacity to the rooms that need it. The duct system sets the ceiling on what the equipment can achieve.

Duct installation is distinct from ductless setups. Ductless systems route refrigerant lines directly to wall-mounted air handlers, eliminating the duct network entirely. A ductless setup is the right answer for additions, garages, or buildings where running new duct runs is not practical. For homes that already have a forced-air layout, new ductwork fabrication is the standard path when replacing or upgrading the central system.

Once a new system is running, the interior of the duct network collects dust, debris, and in Florida's climate, mold spores. Air duct cleaning removes that buildup and is recommended after construction or renovation work that generates fine particulate inside the structure.

Signs that a home needs new duct runs

Ductwork installation becomes necessary when the existing distribution system can no longer support the equipment or the structure it serves. Several conditions point to a failing or inadequate duct layout.

  • Uneven temperatures: Uneven temperatures between rooms often trace back to undersized branch runs or collapsed flex sections that restrict airflow to specific zones.
  • High energy use: High energy use with no change in habits signals that the air handler is working against duct resistance, leakage, or both.
  • Visible damage: Visible damage such as disconnected joints, crushed flex, or deteriorating duct board means conditioned air is escaping before it reaches the living space.
  • System replacement: System replacement is the most common trigger for new ductwork fabrication, since older duct layouts are often sized for equipment that no longer matches current load calculations.
  • Renovation or addition: Renovation or addition work that changes the floor plan requires new branch runs to serve the modified or expanded spaces.

An air conditioning contractor evaluates the existing layout before recommending full replacement versus targeted repairs, since partial re-runs are sometimes sufficient when the trunk lines are still in good condition.

Ductwork installation in Clearwater and what to expect from the process

Ductwork installation in Clearwater follows the Manual D sizing standard used across Florida. Local climate adds specific requirements. Attic temperatures in Clearwater regularly exceed 130 degrees Fahrenheit in summer, which means uninsulated duct runs lose a significant portion of their cooling capacity before the air reaches the register. Every supply run installed in an unconditioned attic requires a minimum R-6 insulation wrap to meet Florida energy code.

The installation sequence starts with the trunk line, which runs from the air handler toward the far end of the structure. Branch runs are then cut in and routed to each register location. Flex duct makes the final connection to the boot. After the runs are complete, the system is balanced by adjusting dampers and register sizes until airflow at each outlet matches the design CFM.
