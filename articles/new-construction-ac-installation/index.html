What new construction AC installation is

New Construction AC Installation is the process of designing, placing and commissioning a cooling system inside a building that has not yet been occupied, with work staged across two distinct building phases: rough-in and trim-out. Rough-in covers ductwork layout, line set runs and electrical stub-outs completed before drywall closes the walls. Trim-out covers equipment mounting, refrigerant charging and thermostat wiring once the interior is finished. The most common system types installed in new residential construction are split-systems and mini-splits, though packaged units appear on slab-on-grade builds where attic space is limited. Because the walls are open during rough-in, a contractor can route ducts and line sets along the most efficient path rather than fishing through finished cavities. Measurable attributes of new construction AC installation

New Construction AC Installation carries several measurable attributes that distinguish it from other HVAC work. Capacity is sized to the structure. A load calculation determines the appropriate tonnage, with residential projects typically falling between 1.5 and 5 tons. The installation phase spans from pre-drywall rough-in through final inspection, requiring the contractor to be on-site at two separate construction milestones. Coordination is multi-trade: the HVAC crew must sequence with framers, electricians, and plumbers so rough-in is complete before insulation and drywall close the cavities. Building phase determines what work is performed on each visit, with rough-in covering ductwork and line sets and trim-out covering equipment startup and commissioning. These attributes together define the scope and scheduling complexity that separates a new build AC install from a straightforward swap-out.

  • System capacity: System capacity ranges from 1.5 to 5 or more tons, set by a load calculation tied to the building's square footage, insulation and window area.
  • Installation phase: Installation phase runs from pre-drywall rough-in to final inspection, requiring at least two coordinated site visits at different construction milestones.
  • Coordination requirement: Coordination requirement is multi-trade scheduling, where the HVAC contractor sequences with framers, electricians and plumbers before walls close.
  • Building phase: Building phase divides the work into rough-in (ductwork, line sets, electrical stub-outs) and trim-out (equipment mounting, refrigerant charge, thermostat wiring).
  • System type: System type is most often a split-system or mini-split on residential new construction, with packaged units used where attic space is not available.

New construction AC installation vs. Retrofit and replacement work

New Construction AC Installation is not the same as Retrofit AC Installation, Replacement AC Installation or Existing Home AC Replacement, and the differences are practical, not merely definitional. Retrofit and replacement work happens in occupied or finished structures, where the contractor must work around existing walls, ceilings and ductwork rather than setting the layout from scratch. A new build AC install sets duct routes, equipment locations and line set paths before drywall, so the system fits the building rather than the building constraining the system. AC unit replacement is the most common contrast: it swaps equipment on an existing refrigerant circuit and duct system, while new construction installation builds that circuit and system from nothing. AC repair is a further contrast, addressing faults in an already-operating system rather than commissioning a new one. Knowing which category a project falls into determines permitting requirements, scheduling, and the scope of work a contractor must price. New construction HVAC installation checklist: phases and coordination points

A new construction HVAC installation checklist covers the tasks and sign-offs required at each phase of the build. Timing is critical. The rough-in phase must be complete and inspected before insulation and drywall proceed, so the checklist front-loads the most time-sensitive items first. Trim-out and commissioning happen near the end of construction, after interior finishes are in place, and include the refrigerant charge, airflow balancing, and thermostat programming. Coordination with general contractors in Clearwater helps keep each phase on schedule and avoid delays at inspection.

  • Pre-construction load calculation: Load calculation determines system capacity in tons before any equipment is ordered or ductwork is laid out.
  • Permit pull: Permit pull requirements can vary and should be confirmed with the local building authority before rough-in begins.
  • Rough-in ductwork layout: Rough-in ductwork layout sets supply and return runs through open framing, sized to the load calculation.
  • Refrigerant line set placement: Refrigerant line set placement routes copper lines between the air handler and condenser pad before walls close.
  • Electrical rough-in coordination: Electrical rough-in coordination confirms that disconnect boxes and circuit breakers are in place before the HVAC crew returns for trim-out.
  • Equipment pad and air handler setting: Equipment pad and air handler setting positions the outdoor condenser and indoor unit at trim-out, after interior finishes are complete.
  • Refrigerant charge and startup: Refrigerant charge and startup commissions the system, verifies pressures and confirms airflow at every register.
  • Final inspection sign-off: Final inspection requirements can vary and should be confirmed with the local building authority to confirm the installed system meets local mechanical code before occupancy.
