# Capacitor Failure: A Poulin Cooling A/C and Refrigeration Reference Guide

## Capacitor failure in Clearwater

Capacitor failure occurs when a run or start capacitor loses its ability to store and release an electrical charge. This condition leaves the motors in an air conditioning system unable to start or run properly. A bad AC capacitor ranks among the most common reasons a cooling system stops operating on a hot Florida afternoon. Capacitor failure belongs to the category of Electrical Component Failure, sharing the same diagnostic group as [contactor failure](https://poulincoolingacandrefrigeration.mintlify.app/contactor-failure) and similar electrical issues that interrupt normal system function.

### What capacitor failure is and how it differs from a healthy capacitor

Capacitor failure exists when a capacitor can no longer maintain its rated capacitance, which is measured in microfarads (µF). A healthy capacitor shows a reading within roughly five percent of its rated value on a capacitance meter. A weak capacitor falls outside that tolerance range, while a blown capacitor measures near zero or shows an open circuit. The difference determines whether a unit starts its compressor and fan motors within seconds or instead hums, trips a breaker, or refuses to start. Rated capacitance serves as the first value every technician verifies during an [AC repair](https://poulincoolingacandrefrigeration.mintlify.app/ac-repair) diagnosis.

### Measurable attributes of capacitor failure

Capacitor failure presents three measurable attributes that establish its severity and direct the repair approach.

- **Capacitance drift:** Capacitance drift is the percentage by which a capacitor's measured value has fallen from its rated value. A drift beyond five percent typically causes motor stress; a drift beyond twenty percent usually prevents starting entirely.

- **Age at failure:** Age at failure for a run or start capacitor ranges from five to fifteen years, depending on heat exposure, voltage fluctuations, and duty cycle. Florida's year-round cooling season pushes capacitors toward the lower end of that range.

- **Failure mode:** Failure mode describes how the capacitor gave out. The three modes are weak (reduced capacitance but still functional), open (circuit broken, no charge stored), and shorted (internal short that can damage connected motors).

### AC capacitor failure symptoms to recognize

Capacitor failure produces a consistent pattern of symptoms that become more pronounced as the component degrades. The most frequent indication is a humming sound from the outdoor unit while neither the fan nor compressor moves. A system that starts for a short time before shutting off on a high-pressure fault often contains a weak capacitor that places excessive strain on the compressor. Additional signs include a fan blade that turns only after a manual push, repeated attempts required before the unit starts, and a clear reduction in cooling performance despite a low thermostat setting. A shorted capacitor can generate a burning smell near the outdoor cabinet.

- **Humming with no motor movement:** Humming with no motor movement means the motor is receiving voltage but lacks the phase-shifted current the capacitor normally supplies to create starting torque.

- **Intermittent starting:** Intermittent starting points to a weak capacitor that occasionally delivers enough charge to start the motor but fails under thermal load.

- **Fan spinning only with a push:** A fan that spins only with a manual push is a classic sign of a failed start capacitor on the condenser fan motor.

- **Burning smell or visible bulging:** A burning smell or a casing that is visibly bulging or leaking dielectric fluid confirms the capacitor has shorted or vented internally.

### How capacitor failure relates to compressors and the broader AC system

Capacitor failure stops compressors from starting because the compressor motor relies on the run capacitor to keep the required phase difference between its start and run windings. Without that phase shift, the motor pulls locked-rotor amperage, several times higher than its normal running current, for the entire duration of the start attempt. Prolonged locked-rotor current overheats the motor windings and speeds up insulation breakdown. A compressor subjected to hard-starting against a weak capacitor for one or two seasons frequently displays winding resistance values outside specification when a technician finally measures them. Early replacement of the bad AC capacitor helps protect the compressor, the most expensive component in the outdoor unit.

The dual capacitor, the type most often installed in residential condensing units, powers both the compressor and the condenser fan motor from one cylindrical case. A failure in either section of a dual capacitor affects both components, so a failed fan motor section places added stress on the compressor side. Testing a dual capacitor requires separate checks of the compressor and fan motor sections, which prevents an incomplete failure from going unnoticed. ### How to replace AC capacitor safely and what the repair involves

Capacitor replacement requires specific safety procedures that begin with disconnecting power at the disconnect box and confirming zero voltage at the capacitor terminals with a meter. Capacitors can retain a lethal charge after power removal. The old capacitor must be discharged through a resistor before any wires are disconnected. The replacement capacitor must match the original µF rating and voltage rating exactly. A capacitor with a lower voltage rating than the circuit supplies will fail early. An incorrect µF value causes the motor to run hot or prevents it from starting. After installation, the technician conducts a capacitor test on the new part under load to verify that it maintains rated capacitance within tolerance.

Repair time depends on the specific failure, yet a dual capacitor replacement can often be completed in a short time when the correct part is stocked on the service vehicle.

### Where capacitor failure applies across AC equipment types

Capacitor failure occurs in any AC equipment that uses single-phase induction motors. This description matches the vast majority of residential and light-commercial systems in Clearwater. Split systems, package units, mini-splits with single-phase outdoor units, and heat pumps all employ run capacitors on their compressor and fan motors. Start capacitors appear on older or higher-torque compressors that require additional torque to overcome initial inertia. Window units and portable air conditioners contain smaller capacitors that fail through identical mechanisms. Three-phase commercial equipment experiences this failure less often. Three-phase motors produce their own starting torque without capacitors. These systems still use capacitors for power factor correction, and those units remain subject to failure.

Equipment type determines the initial focus of the technician. Knowledge of the system layout before the cabinet is opened reduces diagnostic time and lowers the risk of overlooking an additional failed component next to the primary blown capacitor. A residential split system normally contains one dual capacitor in the outdoor unit. A package unit often uses separate run capacitors for the supply fan motor, condenser fan motor, and compressor. Awareness of these layout differences enables the technician to locate the correct capacitor without delay and evaluate all related parts during a single inspection. Technicians who identify the system layout before opening the cabinet can often check every relevant capacitor in one inspection.
