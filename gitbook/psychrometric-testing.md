### Scope and component parts of the psychrometric testing process

The psychrometric testing process includes every measurable air property that shows whether a cooling system performs its intended function. A technician records readings at the supply and return air registers. Those values go onto a psychrometric chart. The technician then determines where the operating point sits relative to the design condition. The process consists of humidity sensors, thermometers, barometers, psychrometric chart analysis, and performance interpretation. Each element supplies a separate data layer. Combined, they deliver information that visual inspection cannot supply.

Humidity testing supplies one of the most informative parts of the process.[HVAC system diagnostics](system-diagnostics.md) that omit humidity measurement miss roughly half the picture of indoor comfort. Psychrometric testing produces six primary measurements. Each measurement answers a separate question about air condition. The attributes and what they reveal appear below.

- **Dry bulb temperature:** Dry bulb temperature is the standard air temperature reading in °F or °C, taken with a thermometer shielded from radiant heat.

- **Wet bulb temperature:** Wet bulb temperature is measured with a thermometer whose sensing tip is wrapped in a water-saturated wick. The evaporation rate of that water reflects how much moisture the air holds, making wet bulb temperature the key input for calculating relative humidity and enthalpy.

- **Dew point temperature:** Dew point temperature is the temperature at which air becomes saturated and moisture begins to condense.

- **Relative humidity:** Relative humidity is the ratio of actual moisture in the air to the maximum moisture the air can hold at that temperature, expressed as a percentage.

- **Enthalpy:** Enthalpy is the total heat content of the air, combining sensible heat and latent heat into one value in BTU per pound of dry air. The difference in enthalpy between return and supply air tells a technician exactly how much total cooling the system is delivering.

- **Grains of moisture:** Grains of moisture per pound of dry air is the absolute humidity value read directly off a psychrometric chart once dry bulb and wet bulb temperatures are plotted. It does not change with temperature the way relative humidity does, making it the stable reference for dehumidification calculations.

### What psychrometric testing is not

Psychrometric testing differs from visual inspection, a quick system check and trial-and-error repair. The distinction affects diagnosis accuracy. Visual inspection confirms whether components exist and remain undamaged. A quick system check confirms whether the unit turns on and moves air. Trial-and-error repair replaces parts according to symptom patterns instead of measured data. None of these methods generates the air property values required to diagnose latent load problems, oversized equipment short-cycling or duct leakage that appears only as a humidity deviation.

A system can pass every visual check and still deliver air at 65 percent relative humidity because the coil runs too warm to condense sufficient moisture. Only wet bulb and dew point measurements show that condition. Humidity testing replaces guesswork with a number.

### Common problems psychrometric testing identifies in Clearwater

Psychrometric testing identifies problems that Clearwater's climate makes especially common because of the region's sustained high outdoor humidity from spring through fall. The problems listed below appear regularly in AC system diagnostics for both residential and commercial buildings.

- **High indoor humidity despite cooling:** High indoor humidity despite cooling points to an oversized system that short-cycles, pulling the dry bulb temperature down quickly but running too briefly to remove latent moisture from the air.

- **Enthalpy mismatch:** Enthalpy mismatch between design and measured values reveals duct leakage returning unconditioned attic air into the system, a problem invisible to pressure testing alone.

- **Coil freeze risk:** Coil freeze risk appears when wet bulb temperature at the evaporator inlet drops below the threshold where condensate begins to freeze rather than drain, a condition that damages the coil and blocks airflow within hours.
