## AC water leak in Clearwater - Poulin Cooling A/C and Refrigeration

An AC water leak, called an AC condensation leak, is unintended water escaping from an air conditioning system. It occurs through a failed drain line, cracked condensate pan, or frosted evaporator coil that thaws faster than the drain can handle. The system pulls humidity from indoor air as it cools. That moisture normally drips into a pan and exits through a drain line to the outside. When any part of that path fails, water pools around the air handler or saturates walls and ceilings. AC water leak is a type of AC System Malfunction, meaning it signals a breakdown in the system's normal moisture-management function rather than a refrigerant or electrical fault. ### Components involved in an AC water leak

The condensate pan, the drain line, and the evaporator coil form the components involved in an AC water leak. The condensate pan sits directly beneath the evaporator coil and collects dripping moisture; a crack or rust hole in the pan lets water reach the subfloor before it ever reaches the drain. The drain line carries water from the pan to an exterior drain point, and a clog from algae, dust, or debris is the single most common cause of an air conditioner drip indoors. The evaporator coil freezes when airflow drops too low or refrigerant charge is off, and the resulting ice melts in a volume the pan cannot absorb quickly enough. ### Causes of AC water leak and measurable attributes

The causes of AC water leak fall into two categories: drainage failures and coil failures. Drainage failures include clogged or disconnected drain lines, a tilted air handler that prevents water from reaching the drain port, and a corroded condensate pan. Coil failures include ice buildup from low refrigerant or a dirty air filter blocking airflow across the coil. Each cause is measurable. Specific attributes help a technician diagnose the source. ### How an AC water leak relates to the broader AC system

Left unaddressed, a water leak accelerates other failures: standing water in the condensate pan corrodes the pan itself, and persistent moisture around the air handler promotes mold growth in the ductwork. A water leak forces the system to work harder to maintain setpoint temperatures because a frozen coil transfers heat poorly, which drives up electricity draw. ### Signs of an AC water leak

AC water leak signs range from obvious pooling to subtle performance changes. The most direct sign is standing water or a water stain near the indoor air handler or on the ceiling below it. A musty smell from the vents can follow a drain line clog, as stagnant water in the pan begins to support mold and algae. Some systems include a float switch in the condensate pan that shuts the unit off when water reaches a set level; a system that turns off for no apparent reason on a hot day is often tripping that switch. Reduced cooling output on a humid day, combined with ice visible on the refrigerant lines near the air handler, points to a coil freeze that will produce a heavy air conditioner drip once the system defrosts. ### AC water leak versus normal AC function

AC water leak is the direct opposite of dry AC operation and normal AC function. In normal AC function, condensate forms on the evaporator coil, drips into the pan at a controlled rate, and exits through the drain line without accumulating indoors. The system removes humidity. No water is deposited anywhere inside the structure. An AC condensation leak breaks that closed path: water exits somewhere other than the intended drain point, and the indoor environment gains moisture instead of losing it. The distinction matters for diagnosis because a technician confirming normal AC function will find a dry air handler cabinet, a clear drain line with visible flow at the exterior exit point, and an evaporator coil that is cold and wet on its surface but not frozen. Any deviation from those three conditions is a sign of a developing or active AC water leak.
