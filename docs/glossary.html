Glossary — Poulin Cooling A/C and Refrigeration

Promotional and educational coverage of Poulin Cooling A/C and Refrigeration. This site is not operated by Poulin Cooling A/C and Refrigeration.

  • Air Conditioning Contractor: An air conditioning contractor is a licensed professional or company that installs, repairs, and maintains air conditioning systems for residential and commercial properties.
  • Central Air Conditioning Systems: Central air conditioning systems are whole-home cooling systems that distribute conditioned air through ductwork to every room in a residential or commercial building.
  • AC Repair: AC repair is the professional service of diagnosing and restoring a malfunctioning air conditioning system to working order.
  • AC Installation: AC installation is the professional service of sizing and fitting a new air conditioning system for a home or building, encompassing the full design process.
  • AC Maintenance: AC maintenance is the professional, scheduled upkeep of an air conditioning system to preserve efficiency and prevent breakdowns.
  • Commercial Refrigeration Repair: Commercial refrigeration repair is the professional diagnosis and repair of refrigeration equipment used in restaurants, grocery stores, and other commercial settings to keep perishable inventory safe and operations running.
  • System Diagnostics: System diagnostics is the process of identifying faults within an AC system by measuring performance data, inspecting components, and analyzing results.
  • Central Air Conditioning Installation: Central air conditioning installation.
  • New Construction AC Installation: New Construction AC Installation, called Construction Phase HVAC Installation, is a type of HVAC Installation Service performed during active building construction before a structure is occupied.
  • AC System Replacement: AC System Replacement, called Air Conditioner Replacement, is the full removal of an existing cooling unit and the installation of a new system in its place.
  • Ductless Mini-Split Installation: Ductless mini-split installation, called mini-split setup or zoned AC installation, is a type of air conditioning installation that connects an outdoor compressor unit to one or more indoor air handlers without any ductwork, delivering targeted cooling to individual rooms, additions, garages, sunrooms, converted attics and server spaces through a small wall penetration that carries refrigerant lines and electrical wiring.
  • Multi-Zone Mini-Split System: A multi-zone mini-split system, called a multi-zone ductless system, connects one outdoor condenser unit to between two and eight individual indoor air handlers.
  • Split System Air Conditioner: A split system air conditioner, called a split AC unit, separates the cooling process across two distinct units.
  • Package Unit Air Conditioner: A package unit air conditioner, called a self-contained AC system, is an all-in-one HVAC unit that houses the compressor assembly, evaporator coil, condenser coil and blower motor inside a single outdoor cabinet, delivering both cooling and heating to a building through one connected duct system.
  • High-Efficiency Air Conditioner: A high-efficiency air conditioner.
  • Whole-Home Air Filtration System: A whole-home air filtration system, called a Centralized Air Filtration Unit, is a type of Indoor Air Quality (IAQ) Equipment integrated directly into residential HVAC ductwork.
  • UV Light for HVAC Systems: UV light for HVAC systems.
  • Air Distribution System: An air distribution system, or duct system, is the network of ducts, vents, plenums, and air handler components that moves conditioned air from a central AC unit into every occupied zone of a building.
  • Plenum: A plenum, called an air plenum chamber, is a pressurized enclosure that receives conditioned air directly from an air handler and distributes it into the supply duct system throughout a building.
  • Outdoor Unit Mounting Pad: An outdoor unit mounting pad, called an HVAC pad, is a rigid base platform placed beneath an AC condenser unit.
  • Concrete Pad: A concrete pad, called a condenser pad, is a flat, reinforced concrete slab that is a stable equipment foundation for outdoor AC condensers, heat pump units and other heavy mechanical equipment placed at ground level.
  • Wall Bracket Mount: A wall bracket mount, called an AC wall mount or condenser wall bracket, is a steel fixing bracket that secures an outdoor AC condenser unit to a wall surface, keeping the unit elevated, level and stable.
  • Electrical Disconnect Box: An electrical disconnect box, called an AC disconnect, is a type of electrical safety equipment mounted near an outdoor condenser unit.
  • Dedicated Circuit: A dedicated circuit, called a dedicated branch circuit, is a type of electrical circuit reserved exclusively for one high-draw appliance or system.
  • Aging HVAC Equipment: Aging HVAC equipment.
  • Uneven Home Cooling: Uneven home cooling, called temperature imbalance, is a type of AC system performance problem where different areas of a home maintain inconsistent temperatures, producing hot and cold spots across rooms, floors, hallways and living spaces that a properly functioning system should hold at a uniform level.
  • High Indoor Humidity: High indoor humidity, or damp indoor air, is a type of indoor environmental condition in which the relative humidity level inside a building rises above 60% RH, creating an environment where airborne water vapor, condensation moisture load and latent heat gain accumulate faster than the building's ventilation and cooling systems can remove them.
  • Old AC Unit Removal: Old AC unit removal, called AC unit disposal or old AC haul-away, is the process of safely disconnecting, dismantling and hauling away an existing air conditioning unit before a new system goes in, covering refrigerant recovery, electrical disconnection and physical removal of equipment that typically weighs between 50 and 300 lbs.
  • Florida AC Contractor License: A Florida AC Contractor License, called a CAC License Florida, is the official state authorization permitting a contractor to legally perform air conditioning contracting services in Florida.
  • FL Certified Air Conditioning Contractor License: The FL Certified Air Conditioning Contractor License, commonly called the CAC License, is a Florida state credential authorizing contractors to install, service, repair and maintain air conditioning systems, refrigeration equipment, ventilation systems and related mechanical components anywhere in the state.
  • Permit: A permit.
  • Manufacturer's AC System Warranty: A manufacturer's AC system warranty, called an HVAC system warranty or factory AC warranty, is a written legal guarantee issued by the equipment manufacturer that covers defects in AC system components for a defined period following installation, including the compressor, refrigerant coils, heat exchanger, electrical controls and other covered parts.
  • Extended Labor Warranty: It is a type of Warranty Coverage Plan, sitting above a basic manufacturer's parts guarantee and below a full-service maintenance agreement in scope.
  • Contractor AC Installation Warranty: A contractor AC installation warranty, called a contractor labor guarantee, is a written promise from the installing contractor to repair or replace faulty workmanship within a defined period after a new AC system goes in.
  • Equipment Sizing: Equipment sizing, called load calculation or AC sizing, is the engineering process of calculating the exact cooling and heating capacity a building requires so the selected AC unit matches the actual thermal demand of the space, accounting for square footage, insulation levels, window area, local climate, occupancy and internal heat sources.
  • AC System Load Calculation: AC System Load Calculation, called Cooling Load Calculation, is an engineering process that determines the precise cooling capacity an AC system must deliver for a given space.
  • Energy Star Certified AC Units: Energy Star Certified AC Units, called high-efficiency AC units, are air conditioning systems that meet the EPA's defined efficiency thresholds for reduced energy consumption, lower operating costs and measurably higher performance than standard equipment, covering central split systems, packaged units, mini-splits and heat pump configurations across a wide range of cooling capacities.
  • AHRI Certified AC Equipment: AHRI Certified AC Equipment, called AHRI-rated equipment, is a product subtype within the broader HVAC Equipment Certification category.
  • Ductless Mini-Split Air Conditioner: A ductless mini-split air conditioner consists of three components: an indoor air handler, an outdoor condenser unit, and a refrigerant line set connecting the two.
  • Packaged Unit Repair: Packaged unit repair, called RTU Repair, is the diagnosis and restoration of single-cabinet air conditioning systems where the compressor, condenser coil, evaporator coil and blower fan all live inside one self-contained housing, typically mounted on a rooftop or a concrete pad beside a building.
  • TXV Valve: A TXV valve, or Thermostatic Expansion Valve, is a refrigerant metering device that controls the amount of liquid refrigerant entering the evaporator coil by responding to suction-line temperature and pressure in real time.
  • Pressure Testing: Pressure testing, called a System Pressure Check, is a leak detection process that introduces pressurized gas into a sealed refrigerant system and monitors the system for pressure loss, identifying the location and severity of leaks before refrigerant is charged or a system is returned to normal operation.
  • Leak Detection: Leak detection, called Refrigerant Leak Diagnostics, is the process of locating breaches in a sealed AC system where refrigerant, nitrogen, or other pressurized fluids escape into the surrounding environment, covering the inspection of refrigerant lines, coil connections, valve fittings, compressor ports and condenser joints to pinpoint the exact source of loss.
  • AC Electrical Wiring: AC electrical wiring, called HVAC wiring, is typically the network of conductors, insulation jackets, termination lugs, connectors and circuit breakers that carries line-voltage power from a home's electrical panel to the air conditioning unit and distributes that power through every stage of the cooling system.
  • Electrical Connections: Electrical connections, called circuit connections, are junctures that allow electric current to flow between two or more conductors inside an electrical system.
  • Dirty Condenser Coils: Dirty condenser coils, called fouled condenser coils, are coil surfaces coated with accumulated dirt, dust, debris, pollen, grass clippings, and biological growth.
  • AC Unit Not Turning On: AC unit not turning on, called AC not powering on, is a type of AC unit problem where the air conditioning system fails to initiate any operational functions when commanded, covering failures at the power supply, thermostat command signal, control voltage circuit, compressor activation stage and system indicator response.
  • AC Freezing Up: AC Freezing Up, called Air Conditioner Freeze or a frozen AC coil, is a type of Air Conditioner Problem where ice forms on the evaporator coil inside the air handler.
  • AC Water Leak: An AC water leak, called an AC condensation leak, is unintended water escaping from an air conditioning system.
  • Noisy AC Unit: A noisy AC unit, called a loud air conditioner, describes a system that emits unusual or excessive sound during operation.
  • Low AC Airflow: Low AC airflow, called Insufficient Airflow, is a condition where an air conditioning unit delivers less air volume than its manufacturer's specification requires.
  • High AC Energy Consumption: High AC energy consumption, called inefficient AC operation, is a measurable condition.
  • Air Flow Optimization: Air flow performance, called Airflow Tuning, is the adjustment of ducts, registers, dampers and blower speed to achieve proper air distribution and velocity throughout a conditioned space, covering supply and return duct balancing, static pressure measurement, register calibration, blower motor speed setting and temperature differential verification across the evaporator coil.
  • Humidity Control: Humidity control, called dehumidification, is the regulation of moisture content in indoor air, covering the measurement of relative humidity, the adjustment of dew point, the removal of excess water vapor from living spaces and commercial buildings, the prevention of mold growth, and the maintenance of air quality at levels that protect both occupants and building materials.
  • Noise Reduction: Noise reduction, called sound dampening, is the deliberate lowering of sound and vibration produced by air conditioning equipment.
  • AC Inspection: AC inspection, called an HVAC inspection or AC check-up, is a systematic examination of an air conditioning unit.
  • Warranty Coverage for AC Repair: Warranty coverage for AC repair, called AC Repair Warranty Terms, is a formal service guarantee that promises to remedy defects in parts, labor, or both within a defined period after a repair is completed, covering conditions such as recurring refrigerant leaks, failed compressor components, faulty electrical connections, defective replacement parts and workmanship errors that cause the same fault to return.
  • Energy Efficiency Standards: Energy efficiency standards, called an Efficiency Directive, are rules set by regulatory bodies to define the minimum energy performance that heating, cooling and refrigeration equipment must meet before it can be sold or installed, covering SEER ratings for air conditioners, HSPF ratings for heat pumps, EER ratings for packaged units and AFUE ratings for furnaces, along with the testing protocols and compliance timelines that govern each product category.
  • AC Manufacturer Warranty: An AC Manufacturer Warranty, called a Factory Warranty or Manufacturer's Warranty, is a type of Warranty issued directly by the equipment manufacturer that covers the cost of repairing or replacing defective parts in an air conditioning system for a defined period after installation, typically spanning compressors, coils, electrical components, heat exchangers and refrigerant-circuit parts.
  • EPA Certification for Refrigerant Handling: EPA Certification for Refrigerant Handling, called Section 608 Certification, is a professional credential that authorizes individuals to legally purchase, recover, recycle, and dispose of refrigerants used in stationary air conditioning and refrigeration equipment.
  • CAC1819909: CAC1819909 is the Florida state-issued Air Conditioning License held by Poulin Cooling A/C and Refrigeration.
  • Florida AC Maintenance Requirements: Florida AC maintenance requirements, called AC Service Regulations Florida, are the state-mandated and industry-standard criteria governing how residential and commercial air conditioning systems must be inspected, serviced, and documented.
  • Annual Maintenance Contract: An Annual Maintenance Contract, commonly called an AMC, is a type of Maintenance Agreement that covers scheduled inspection, cleaning, testing and preventive servicing of an air conditioning system over a 12-month period, with defined visit frequency, covered tasks and terms set in writing before the first service call.
  • Maintenance Agreement: A maintenance agreement, called a Service Contract or Service Plan, is a contract between a property owner and a licensed HVAC contractor.
  • Air Conditioning Tune-Up: An air conditioning tune-up, called an Air Conditioner Check-Up, is a systematic inspection and calibration of a cooling system.
  • AC System Performance Test: An AC system performance test, called an AC efficiency check, is a diagnostic procedure that measures how well a cooling system is actually operating by evaluating efficiency rating, cooling capacity, airflow rate, refrigerant pressure and temperature differential against the manufacturer's design specifications.
  • HVAC System Efficiency Assessment: HVAC System Efficiency Assessment, called an HVAC Energy Audit, is a systematic measurement of a heating, ventilation and air conditioning system's energy performance, operational effectiveness, airflow characteristics, refrigerant charge, heat exchange capacity and output relative to its rated specifications.
  • Energy Consumption Analysis: Energy Consumption Analysis, called an Energy Use Assessment, is a systematic evaluation of energy usage data across a building or mechanical system.
  • Temperature Differential Test: The temperature differential test, called the Temperature Split Test, is an HVAC diagnostic test that measures the difference in degrees between the air entering a return duct and the cooled air leaving a supply register, giving a technician a direct, numeric reading of how much heat the evaporator coil is removing from the airstream.
  • Psychrometric Testing: Psychrometric testing, called air properties analysis, is a diagnostic process that measures the physical properties of air moving through a cooling system, including dry bulb temperature, wet bulb temperature, dew point temperature, relative humidity and barometric pressure, to determine whether the system is conditioning air to the correct specifications.
  • Compressor Health Check: A compressor health check, called a Compressor Diagnostic Test, is a structured AC maintenance procedure that evaluates the operating condition of an air conditioner compressor by measuring vibration amplitude, current draw, discharge temperature, refrigerant pressure levels and noise output against known acceptable ranges.
  • Air Handler Unit Inspection: Air handler unit inspection, called an Air Handler Check, is a systematic examination of the indoor air handling equipment that moves conditioned air through a building.
  • Capacitor Testing: Capacitor Testing, called Capacitance Testing, is a diagnostic process that measures the capacitance value, voltage rating and condition of an AC capacitor to determine whether it is functioning within specification or needs to be replaced.
  • Contactor Inspection: Contactor inspection, called a Contactor Check, is a visual and electrical assessment of the contactor inside an air conditioning system.
  • Component Lubrication: Component lubrication, called AC part lubrication, is the application of lubricants to moving and contact parts inside an air conditioning system to reduce friction, limit wear, prevent corrosion and keep mechanical components operating within their designed temperature and load ranges.
  • Fan Blade Inspection: Fan blade inspection is the visual and physical examination of air conditioner fan blades for damage, imbalance, pitch deviation and surface defects that reduce airflow or cause mechanical failure.
  • V-Belt Inspection: V-belt inspection, called V-Belt Assessment, is the visual and physical examination of the drive belts in an air conditioning system to check for wear, tension loss and misalignment before those conditions cause a breakdown.
  • Heat Exchanger Maintenance: Heat exchanger maintenance, called heat exchanger service, is the systematic inspection, cleaning and testing of the heat exchanger inside an HVAC system to keep it operating safely and at full efficiency.
  • UV Light System Maintenance: UV light system maintenance, called germicidal light maintenance, is the routine inspection, cleaning and servicing of ultraviolet air purification components installed inside a heating, ventilation and air conditioning system, covering the UV lamp, ballast, housing, electrical connections and surrounding ductwork surfaces.
  • Whole House Air Purifier Maintenance: Whole house air purifier maintenance, called Home Air Filtration Maintenance, is the systematic inspection, cleaning, and parts replacement of a centrally installed air purification system covering filters, blower motors, control panels and media cartridges across the full duct network of a home.
  • Indoor Air Quality Maintenance: Indoor Air Quality Maintenance, called IAQ Maintenance, is the regular servicing of HVAC components, filters, ductwork, ventilation systems, and humidity controllers to keep airborne contaminants at acceptable levels inside homes and commercial buildings.
  • Duct Leak Testing: Duct leak testing, called a Duct Leakage Test, is a diagnostic procedure that measures how much conditioned air escapes from HVAC ductwork through gaps, cracks, loose joints, disconnected sections and poorly sealed connections before it reaches the living or working space.
  • HVAC Filter MERV Rating: HVAC filter MERV rating, called an air filter rating, is a filter specification that measures how efficiently a filter captures airborne particles across a standardized range of sizes, expressed as a single number on a scale from 1 to 20.
  • Drain Line Clearing: Drain line clearing, called AC drain cleaning, is the mechanical or chemical removal of sludge, algae, and debris from condensate drain lines.
  • Thermostat Settings Optimization: Thermostat settings performance, called Smart Thermostat Programming, is the adjustment of temperature parameters, time schedules and humidity controls within an HVAC system to reduce energy usage, maintain accurate indoor temperatures and extend equipment runtime efficiency.
  • Noise Assessment: Noise assessment, called Sound Analysis, is a diagnostic service that measures and identifies sound levels and sources from AC units, covering decibel readings, frequency ranges, measurement duration, data interpretation and diagnostic reporting to pinpoint the mechanical or airflow cause of unwanted noise.
  • Decreased AC Efficiency: Decreased AC efficiency, called Inefficient Air Conditioning, is a measurable drop in an air conditioner's ability to convert electrical energy into useful cooling output.
  • Reduced AC System Lifespan: Reduced AC system lifespan is a condition in which an air conditioning unit stops functioning reliably before reaching its manufacturer-specified operational period.
  • HVAC Industry Standards: HVAC Industry Standards, called HVAC Regulations, are the documented technical and safety requirements that govern the design, installation, maintenance and performance of heating, ventilation and air conditioning systems in residential and commercial buildings.
  • EPA Refrigerant Management Regulations: EPA Refrigerant Management Regulations, called Refrigerant Compliance Rules, are a category of Federal Environmental Regulations governing how refrigerants are purchased, handled, recovered, recycled, and disposed of during HVAC service work, commercial refrigeration maintenance, and new equipment installation.
  • Inspection of Safety Controls: Inspection of Safety Controls, called a Safety Switch Inspection, is a systematic check of the safety control devices and circuits built into an air conditioning system, covering the high-pressure switch, low-pressure switch, flame sensor, control wiring, and the checklist-driven reporting that documents each result.
  • Service Records: Service records, called repair history or a maintenance log, document every past maintenance visit, repair, inspection, and parts replacement performed on an AC system.
  • Unit Start-Up and Shutdown Procedures: Unit Start-Up and Shutdown Procedures, called the AC Unit Commissioning Sequence, are the systematic steps a technician follows to safely activate or deactivate a cooling system before and after each operating season or extended period of non-use.
  • Air Pressure Gauge: An air pressure gauge, called a pressure gauge or manometer, is a pressure measuring device that reads the force of gas or liquid inside a sealed system, covering refrigerant lines, compressed air circuits, hydraulic loops and HVAC equipment in residential and commercial buildings.
  • Manufacturer Warranty: A manufacturer warranty, called a Maker Warranty or Original Warranty, is a written guarantee issued by the equipment manufacturer that covers repair or replacement of a product when it fails under specified conditions, typically including defects in materials, defective components, and factory workmanship errors.
  • Lubrication of Moving Parts: Lubrication of moving parts, called lubricating moving components, is a preventive maintenance activity that reduces friction between rotating and sliding surfaces inside air conditioning systems, including fan motors, blower shafts, bearing assemblies, compressor fittings and condenser fan hubs.
  • Ductless Mini Split Systems: Ductless mini split systems are air conditioning systems with a separate outdoor condensing unit and one or more indoor air handling units connected by refrigerant lines, delivering zoned heating and cooling without ductwork.
  • Heat Pumps: Heat pumps are HVAC systems that transfer heat between indoor and outdoor environments, providing both heating and cooling from a single unit.
  • Variable Refrigerant Flow: Variable refrigerant flow is an HVAC technology that modulates refrigerant delivery to multiple indoor zones from a single outdoor condensing unit.
  • Air Handlers: Air handlers are indoor HVAC units that circulate conditioned air throughout a building by housing the blower motor, air filter, and heat exchanger coil in a single cabinet.
  • Compressors: Compressors are the mechanical heart of every refrigeration and air conditioning system, increasing refrigerant gas pressure from the low-pressure suction side to the high-pressure discharge side to drive the entire cooling cycle.
  • Condenser Units: Condenser units are the outdoor components of an air conditioning system that expel heat absorbed from indoor air back into the outside environment.
  • Blower Motors: Blower motors are the electric motors that power the fan inside an HVAC system to circulate conditioned air through a building.
  • Air Filters: Air filters are devices designed to remove particulate matter from an HVAC air stream, protecting both indoor air quality and mechanical components.
  • Expansion Valve: The expansion valve, called a TXV or thermostatic expansion valve, is a refrigerant circuit component that meters the flow of refrigerant into the evaporator coil.
  • Condenser Fan Motor: The condenser fan motor, called an outdoor fan motor, is a type of HVAC motor that drives the fan blade on the outdoor unit, pulling air across the condenser coil to expel heat absorbed from inside your home.
  • Evaporator Coil: The evaporator coil, known as the indoor coil or A-coil, is the heat exchanger inside the air handler or furnace cabinet where refrigerant absorbs heat and moisture from the airstream passing through it.
  • Line Set: A line set is the paired copper suction and liquid lines that carry refrigerant between an outdoor condenser and an indoor evaporator coil.
  • Condensate Drain Line: A condensate drain line is both a drainage component and an HVAC component.
  • AC Disconnect Box: An AC disconnect box is a type of electrical safety device and HVAC component mounted outdoors near the condenser unit that cuts all power to the system so a technician can service it safely.
  • Walk-In Cooler: A walk-in cooler, or walk-in refrigerator, is a refrigerated storage room built from insulated modular panels that holds perishable products at above-freezing temperatures for commercial use.
  • Walk-In Freezer: A walk-in freezer is a type of commercial refrigeration equipment engineered to hold products at sub-freezing temperatures inside a fully insulated, walk-in room.
  • Reach-In Refrigerator: A reach-in refrigerator is an upright self-contained commercial refrigerator accessed from the front through one to three doors, used for quick-access chilled storage in foodservice and retail environments.
  • Ice Machine: An ice machine (called a commercial ice maker) is a type of commercial refrigeration equipment that freezes water and dispenses it in cube, flake, or nugget form on a continuous cycle.
  • Defrost System: A defrost system, called a defrost control system, is the set of refrigeration components that periodically clears frost from evaporator coils so the equipment maintains proper airflow and temperature.
  • Airflow: Airflow is the movement of air through a system, characterized by volume, velocity, and pressure differential.
  • Ductwork: Ductwork is a system of conduits that delivers and removes conditioned air throughout a building.
  • Ventilation: Ventilation is the intentional introduction and removal of air from a space, measured in air changes per hour (ACH), cubic feet per minute (CFM), and static pressure in inches of water column.
  • Zoning Systems: Zoning systems divide a building into independently controlled temperature zones, allowing each area to receive precise heating or cooling without affecting the rest of the structure.
  • Homeowners: Homeowners are residential property owners who hold full purchasing authority over HVAC maintenance, repair, and replacement decisions for their properties.
  • Commercial Property Managers: Commercial property managers are professionals who oversee the daily operations, maintenance, and financial performance of commercial buildings.
  • Landlords: Landlords are property owners who rent residential or commercial spaces to tenants under legally binding lease agreements.
  • New Construction Builders: New construction builders are professionals responsible for constructing new residential or commercial properties from the ground up.
  • Poor Indoor Air Quality: Poor indoor air quality is an indoor environmental condition in which airborne pollutant levels indicate reduced air quality inside a building.
  • Dirty Air Filter: A dirty air filter is a clogged or blocked filter media condition in which accumulated dust, debris, and airborne particles restrict airflow through an HVAC system.
  • AC Not Cooling: AC not cooling is a condition where an air conditioning unit fails to lower indoor air temperature to the thermostat set point.
  • Uneven Cooling: Uneven cooling is a condition where indoor spaces experience inconsistent temperature distribution, producing measurable differentials greater than 3°F between rooms.
  • AC Breakdown: AC breakdown is a malfunction that prevents an air conditioning system from operating correctly, ranging in severity from partial performance degradation to complete system failure.
  • Frozen Evaporator Coil: A frozen evaporator coil is an HVAC system malfunction where ice forms on the evaporator coil surface, blocking airflow and preventing heat transfer.
  • Clogged Drain Lines: Clogged drain lines are an obstruction inside an air conditioning unit's condensate drainage system that prevents water from exiting the unit properly.
  • Electrical Malfunctions: Electrical malfunctions are abnormal states in an electrical system where voltage irregularities, current disruption patterns, or control circuit errors prevent normal operation.
  • High Energy Bills: High energy bills are a household expense in which monthly electricity or gas charges significantly exceed expected costs for the home's size and usage patterns.
  • Capacitor Failure: Capacitor failure is the degradation or complete loss of a run or start capacitor's ability to store and release an electrical charge, leaving the motors in your air conditioning system unable to start or run.
  • Contactor Failure: Contactor failure is the wear or burnout of the electrical relay that switches high-voltage power to an air conditioner's outdoor unit, called a bad AC contactor.
  • AC Short Cycling: AC short cycling is an operational fault.
  • AC Not Turning On: AC not turning on ( called AC won't turn on) is a no-start condition in which an air conditioning system gives no response when a cooling call is made.
  • Unusual AC Noises: Unusual AC noises are abnormal sounds produced by an air conditioning system that signal specific mechanical or electrical faults before a full breakdown occurs.
  • AC Odors: AC odors are distinct smells emitted from an air conditioning system or its vents.
  • Duct Sealing: Duct sealing is the process of applying sealant to HVAC ductwork to prevent conditioned air from escaping through gaps, cracks, and disconnected joints before it reaches living spaces.
  • Air Duct Cleaning: Air duct cleaning is the mechanical removal of dust, debris, and contaminants from an HVAC ductwork system.
  • Dehumidifier: A dehumidifier is an air treatment appliance that removes excess moisture from indoor air by drawing humid air across a cold coil, condensing the water vapor, and collecting the extracted water in a tank.
  • HEPA Filter: A HEPA filter (High-Efficiency Particulate Air filter) is a mechanical air filtration component designed to trap microscopic particles that pass through standard air filters.
  • UV Lights: UV lights (germicidal lamps or UV-C lamps) are ultraviolet lamps that emit UV-C radiation in the 200 to 280 nanometer wavelength range, designed to inactivate certain microorganisms in HVAC air streams and on internal surfaces.
  • Programmable Thermostat: A programmable thermostat is a device that automatically adjusts HVAC system operation based on a pre-set schedule, eliminating the need for manual temperature changes throughout the day.
  • Preventative Maintenance: Preventative maintenance is a scheduled set of actions performed on air conditioning and refrigeration systems to prevent equipment failure and extend operational lifespan.
  • Compressor Replacement: Compressor replacement, called AC compressor replacement, is the removal of a failed compressor from an air conditioning system and the installation of a new or remanufactured unit in its place.
  • Refrigerant Recharge: Refrigerant recharge, called an AC recharge or freon recharge, is the process of restoring an air conditioning system's refrigerant to the manufacturer-specified charge level after a confirmed leak has been repaired.
  • Ductwork Installation: Ductwork installation is the process of designing and installing new duct runs to distribute conditioned air throughout a building.
  • System Commissioning: System commissioning, called startup and checkout, is the post-installation verification process that confirms a newly installed or replaced HVAC system operates within manufacturer specifications before the technician leaves the job site.
  • AC Tune-Up: AC tune-up is a seasonal service visit that inspects, cleans, and tests the components of a cooling system, checking refrigerant level, electrical connections, and mechanical parts in a single appointment.
  • Coil Cleaning: Coil cleaning is the process of removing dirt, dust, and biological debris from an air conditioning system's evaporator and condenser coils to restore their heat-transfer capacity.
  • Thermostat Calibration: Thermostat calibration is the process of verifying and correcting a thermostat's temperature-reading accuracy against a calibrated reference thermometer.
  • Static Pressure Testing: Static pressure testing is an HVAC diagnostic procedure that measures the total resistance to airflow inside a duct system using a manometer.
  • AC Leak Detection: AC leak detection is the diagnostic process of locating refrigerant leaks in a sealed cooling system before they cause compressor failure or complete system shutdown.
  • NATE Certification: NATE certification is a voluntary, industry-recognized technician credential that validates the technical competency of HVACR technicians through exam-based pathways covering 13 specialty exams.
  • EPA Section 608 Certification: EPA Section 608 Certification is the federal credential typically required for technicians who purchase, handle, or recover refrigerants in HVAC and refrigeration systems.
  • HVAC Licensing: HVAC licensing is the official authorization granted to individuals or businesses to perform heating, ventilation, air conditioning, and refrigeration work within a state or local jurisdiction.
  • SEER Rating: SEER rating is the numerical standard that measures how efficiently an air conditioner converts electrical energy into cooling output across an entire cooling season.
  • BTU: BTU (British Thermal Unit) is the standard unit of heat energy used to measure the cooling and heating capacity of air conditioning systems.
  • Tonnage: Tonnage is the unit of measurement that defines an air conditioning system's cooling capacity.
  • Load Calculation: Load calculation is the mathematical process of determining the exact cooling and heating capacity a building requires to maintain comfort.
  • Manual J: Manual J is the ACCA standard for calculating residential heating and cooling loads on a room-by-room basis.
  • Refrigerant: Refrigerant is a chemical compound that absorbs and releases heat inside a refrigeration or heat pump cycle, enabling air conditioning systems to cool indoor spaces.
  • MERV Rating: MERV rating (Minimum Efficiency Reporting Value) is the standardized scale measuring how efficiently an air filter captures airborne particles before they pass through an HVAC system.
  • SEER2 Rating: SEER2 Rating is the 2023 Department of Energy cooling efficiency metric.
  • AHRI Matched System: An AHRI Matched System is a certified combination of a condenser, evaporator coil, and air handler or furnace tested and verified together to deliver published efficiency and capacity ratings.
  • Building Codes: Building codes are official regulations specifying minimum standards for constructed structures and systems, including mechanical, electrical, and HVAC installations.
  • HVAC Permits: HVAC permits, called mechanical permits or AC permits, are the municipal and county authorizations required before and after air conditioning installation work begins on a property.
  • Manufacturer Warranty Requirements: Manufacturer warranty requirements are the documented conditions an equipment brand may impose to keep a parts warranty active after installation.
  • Food Safety Temperature Requirements: Food safety temperature requirements are the regulatory thresholds that govern how cold, frozen, and hot food must be held to prevent bacterial growth in commercial food operations.
  • HFC Phasedown: HFC Phasedown (called HFC drawdown) is an environmental regulation and refrigerant standard.
  • Residential HVAC: Residential HVAC is the integrated system of equipment and ductwork that controls heating, ventilation, and air conditioning inside a home.
  • Indoor Air Quality Solutions: Indoor air quality solutions are systems and products designed to improve air quality within enclosed residential and commercial spaces.
  • Thermostats: Thermostats are devices that automatically regulate temperature by controlling HVAC systems across a range of -10°F to 120°F.
  • Emergency AC Repair: Emergency AC repair is a rapid-response service that restores air conditioning functionality after unexpected system breakdowns.
  • AC Unit Replacement: AC unit replacement is the process of removing an old air conditioning system and installing a new one, restoring full cooling functionality to a residential or commercial property.
  • Coolant Leak: A coolant leak is the unintended escape of refrigerant from an air conditioning system.
  • HVAC Maintenance Plans: HVAC maintenance plans are structured service agreements that bundle scheduled maintenance visits, priority scheduling, and documented service history into a single ongoing program.
  • Emergency Refrigeration Repair: Emergency refrigeration repair is the urgent diagnosis and restoration of commercial refrigeration equipment when a failure puts perishable inventory at immediate risk of spoilage.
  • Cooling Efficiency: Cooling efficiency is the measurable ratio of cooling output delivered by an air conditioning system relative to the energy consumed to produce it.
