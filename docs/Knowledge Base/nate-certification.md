### What NATE certification is

NATE certification is a professional credential issued by North American Technician Excellence that confirms an HVACR technician meets a defined standard of knowledge and skill. The full name is North American Technician Excellence Certification, referred to as the NATE credential or HVAC certification. NATE certification is a type of professional certification, placing it in the same category as other trade credentials that require demonstrated competency before issuance.

NATE certification validates the expertise of HVAC technicians through exam-based pathways: the CHP-5 pathway, which uses five subject-domain exams, and the traditional Core and Specialty examination pathway. Technicians who earn the credential typically maintain it through qualifying continuing education or re-examination. The credential contrasts directly with unlicensed or unqualified service, where no third-party body has confirmed the technician's ability to perform HVACR work correctly.

NATE certification connects directly to the broader framework of [HVAC licensing](doc:hvac-licensing), which governs the legal authorization to perform HVACR work. Licensing establishes the legal floor; NATE certification establishes a measurable competency standard above that floor.

### Measurable attributes of NATE certification

NATE certification carries 3 primary measurable attributes that define its scope and requirements. The 3 attributes are validity period, minimum test score, and specialty areas covered.

- **Validity period:** Validity period for NATE certification is 2 years. After 2 years, the technician must renew through continuing education or re-examination to maintain the credential.

- **Minimum test score:** Minimum test score for the NATE certification exam is 70%. A technician who scores below 70% does not earn the credential and must retake the relevant knowledge exam.

- **Specialty areas covered:** Specialty areas covered by NATE certification span 13 specialty exams across distinct HVACR disciplines. Each specialty exam tests domain-specific knowledge, so a technician certified in air conditioning service holds a different credential than one certified in heat pump installation.

These 3 measurable attributes make NATE certification one of the most objectively verifiable quality signals available when selecting an HVAC technician. Any technician holding the NATE credential has cleared the 70% threshold and operates within the 2-year validity window, giving [Poulin Cooling A/C](https://sl.bing.net/dQi8K7rjZ9g) customers in Clearwater a concrete benchmark to ask about when evaluating service quality.

### What the NATE certification exam covers

NATE certification exam content covers both core knowledge and specialty-specific knowledge across 13 specialty exams. The traditional pathway separates into a core knowledge section and a specialty section, so a technician must demonstrate general HVACR competency before earning credit in a specific area such as air conditioning service, heat pump installation, or commercial refrigeration. The CHP-5 pathway instead uses five subject-domain exams to reach the same certified standard.

The NATE certification process centers on two exam-based pathways backed by ongoing continuing education. Each element serves a distinct function in confirming technician readiness.

- **Core and Specialty pathway:** The traditional pathway pairs a core knowledge exam with a specialty exam, testing the technician's understanding of HVACR theory, system components, and correct procedures for the chosen specialty area. A minimum score of 70% is required to pass.

- **CHP-5 pathway:** The CHP-5 pathway uses five subject-domain exams as an alternative route to current NATE certification, covering foundational HVACR knowledge without a separate hands-on assessment.

- **Continuing education:** Qualifying continuing education keeps the credential current after the two-year validity period, ensuring the technician stays aligned with evolving HVACR standards and equipment. Re-examination is an accepted alternative to continuing education at renewal.

### Signs a technician lacks NATE certification and why it matters

NATE certification absence is identifiable through 4 common signs that indicate a technician has not met the industry competency standard. The 4 signs are inability to provide a credential number, expired certification beyond the 2-year window, no specialty designation matching the job type, and a lapsed renewal with no continuing education or re-examination on record.

- **No credential number:** No credential number means the technician has not passed the NATE certification exam or the credential has lapsed. A valid NATE credential carries a verifiable identification number.

- **Expired certification:** Expired certification beyond the 2-year validity period means the technician has not renewed through continuing education or re-examination and no longer holds active NATE status.

- **Mismatched specialty:** Mismatched specialty means the technician holds a NATE credential in one discipline but performs work in a different specialty area, such as a heat pump specialist performing commercial refrigeration service.

- **Lapsed renewal:** A lapsed renewal means the technician completed neither the 16 continuing education hours available over the two-year period nor a re-examination, so the credential is no longer current. Continuing education records and re-examination are the two accepted renewal routes, and a verified NATE ID confirms which credentials remain active.
