### Measurable attributes of AHRI certified AC equipment

AHRI Certified AC Equipment carries at least four measurable efficiency attributes that appear on the certified data sheet. SEER2 is the current primary rating scale. It runs from 13.4 to 26 or higher for residential split systems, and the higher the SEER2, the less electricity the unit consumes per unit of cooling delivered. EER2 is the steady-state efficiency rating measured at a fixed outdoor temperature, giving a snapshot of peak-load performance that SEER2 alone does not capture. On equipment rated under the older scale, SEER ranges from 13 to 26 and EER from 8 to 15, and both figures remain on the certified data sheet for cross-reference. Tested capacity output is the fourth attribute. Expressed in BTU/hr, it confirms that the unit delivers the cooling tonnage the manufacturer claims at rated conditions.

These four numbers together let a licensed technician match equipment to a Manual J load calculation rather than guessing at size. ### Contrasts: AHRI certified vs. Non-certified AC equipment

Non-certified AC equipment and AHRI-verified cooling equipment differ in one fundamental way: independent test data exists for one and not the other. No third party has confirmed the rating. An unverified HVAC equipment unit may carry an efficiency label printed by the manufacturer, but no independent testing has confirmed that the compressor, condenser coil, and evaporator coil combination actually achieves that rating under standardized test conditions. Inefficient AC units that lack certification may underperform their stated SEER, which raises monthly electricity costs and increases compressor workload to meet thermostat demand.

The performance gap affects warranty claims. Many equipment manufacturers tie extended warranty coverage to proper matched-system installation, and a matched system is defined by the AHRI-certified combination of indoor and outdoor components. A homeowner who installs a non-certified or mismatched system may find that a compressor failure falls outside warranty coverage because the combination was never tested and certified as a unit. AHRI-rated equipment removes that ambiguity by documenting the exact matched combination before the first cooling season begins.
