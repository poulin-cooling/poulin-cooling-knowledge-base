## AC inspection in Clearwater - Poulin Cooling A/C and Refrigeration

AC inspection, called an HVAC inspection or AC check-up, is a systematic examination of an air conditioning unit. It covers the thermostat, refrigerant levels, electrical connections, coil condition, and system performance to assess the unit's current state. [Poulin Cooling A/C and Refrigeration](https://maps.app.goo.gl/cZBnDeiuXiDPNVZbA) offers AC inspection throughout Clearwater and the surrounding area. An AC inspection is a type of Diagnostic Service, meaning its purpose is to find what is wrong or what is about to go wrong before a failure occurs. Most units need this examination once a year. Florida's year-round cooling demand, however, often makes twice-yearly checks worthwhile.

### Scope and component parts of an AC inspection

AC inspection covers 15 to 30 individual checkpoints across the major subsystems of a cooling unit. The component parts of a full inspection are listed below.

- **Thermostat check:** Thermostat check confirms the control settings match actual indoor temperature readings and that the unit cycles on and off correctly.

- **Refrigerant level assessment:** Refrigerant level assessment measures the charge against the manufacturer's specification and flags leaks that cause the system to lose cooling capacity over time.

- **Electrical connection inspection:** Electrical connection inspection tests voltage and amperage at the contactor, capacitor and disconnect to catch loose or corroded wiring before it causes a failure.

- **Visual system check:** Visual system check examines the cabinet, coil fins, drain pan and condensate line for physical damage, blockages and biological growth.

- **Performance testing:** Performance testing measures supply and return air temperatures to verify the unit is producing the temperature split the manufacturer specifies.

A licensed technician works through these checkpoints in a single visit, producing a clear picture of the unit's condition. Requirements can vary and should be confirmed with the EPA. ### Measurable attributes: duration, frequency and thoroughness

AC inspection takes 30 to 60 minutes for a standard residential unit, depending on the unit's age and how many checkpoints require closer attention. Frequency is the most important scheduling attribute: annual AC inspection is the industry baseline, and skipping a year allows small faults to compound into larger failures. Thoroughness is measured by checkpoint count, and a complete inspection covers between 15 and 30 distinct items. [System diagnostics](https://poulincoolingacandrefrigeration.mintlify.app/system-diagnostics) that go beyond a standard inspection add electrical load testing and airflow measurement, which extend the visit time but produce more granular data on system health.

### How AC inspection relates to the broader HVAC service system

AC inspection sits inside the broader category of Diagnostic Services and connects directly to the other service types a cooling system requires. An HVAC inspection determines the unit's condition and points toward one of three outcomes: the unit passes and needs only routine maintenance, the unit needs targeted AC repair for a specific fault, or the unit is too degraded to repair economically and needs replacement. That last outcome leads directly to [central air conditioning installation](https://poulincoolingacandrefrigeration.mintlify.app/central-air-conditioning-installation), where a new system is sized and set up. For homes under construction, the inspection baseline is established at startup as part of [new construction AC installation](https://poulincoolingacandrefrigeration.mintlify.app/new-construction-ac-installation), so the system's first recorded condition is documented from day one. ### What AC inspection is not

AC inspection is not AC repair, AC installation or emergency AC repair. The distinction matters because each service has a different scope and trigger. AC repair addresses a known fault after a failure has occurred. Emergency AC repair is reactive, called when the system stops working entirely, often in peak summer heat. AC installation replaces or adds equipment. An HVAC inspection, by contrast, is proactive: it finds faults before they cause a breakdown, which is the opposite of the reactive pattern that emergency calls represent. AC neglect, the absence of any scheduled inspection, is the condition that makes emergency calls far more likely, since small refrigerant losses, loose electrical connections and clogged drain lines go undetected until they cause a full system failure.

### Common problems an AC inspection finds

AC inspection most often surfaces refrigerant undercharge, dirty evaporator or condenser coils, failing capacitors, clogged condensate drain lines, and loose electrical connections. Refrigerant undercharge is particularly common in Florida. The long cooling season puts more hours on the system each year than in cooler climates, accelerating wear on fittings and service valves. A clogged condensate drain line is a frequent finding in humid coastal markets: the drain pan fills, water backs up, and the unit shuts down on a safety float switch. Catching a clogged drain during a scheduled AC check-up takes a few minutes to clear; finding it after the pan overflows and damages a ceiling takes considerably longer to resolve.

### Where AC inspection applies

AC inspection applies to residential split systems, packaged rooftop units, ductless mini-split systems, and light commercial cooling equipment. [Poulin Cooling A/C](https://share.google/M20009BLcxJRzB2FL) handles both residential AC services and commercial AC services in Clearwater. The inspection process scales from a single-family home with one air handler to a small commercial property with multiple zones. The checkpoint list adjusts by equipment type: a ductless system has no duct connections to inspect, while a packaged rooftop unit adds belt tension and economizer damper checks to the standard list. Core purpose stays constant. Regardless of equipment type, an HVAC inspection documents the system's current state so that maintenance, repair, or replacement decisions are based on measured data rather than guesswork.
